## 1. Code on Wages, 2019 (Act 29 of 2019): the text that matters

Commencement: the Telangana G.O. (its "Read" item 3) cites **"Notification No.S.O.5322(E), dated: 21.11.2025 published in the Gazette of India-Extraordinary, Ministry of Labour and Employment regarding the appointed day for commencement of certain provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019"**. VERIFIED (quoted inside the Telangana G.O.). Which provisions it brought into force: see 1.9.

All quotes are from the Gazette text. **VERIFIED** unless marked.

### 1.1 Who is covered

| Provision | Exact text | What it means for Baaki |
|---|---|---|
| s.2(k) "employee" | "any person (other than an apprentice ...), employed on wages by an establishment to do any skilled, semi-skilled or unskilled, manual, operational, supervisory, managerial, administrative, technical or clerical work for hire or reward, whether the terms of employment be express or implied, and also includes a person declared to be an employee by the appropriate Government" | A maid hired orally is still covered ("express or implied"). No written contract needed. |
| s.2(l) "employer" | "a person who employs, whether directly or through any person, or on his behalf or on behalf of any person, one or more employees in his establishment" ... "and includes ... (iii) contractor" | One employee is enough. A placement agency or contractor can also be the "employer". |
| s.2(m) "establishment" | "any place where any industry, trade, business, manufacture or occupation is carried on and includes Government establishment" | **The weak point.** A private home is not obviously such a place. See 1.2. |
| s.2(y) "wages" | "all remuneration ... expressed in terms of money ... and includes,- (i) basic pay; (ii) dearness allowance; and (iii) retaining allowance". Explanation: remuneration in kind counts only up to "fifteen per cent. of the total wages" | Food or a room given to a live-in maid can count for **at most 15%** of wages. "We give her food, so Rs 6,000 is enough" is not a defence. |
| s.2(z) "worker" | "employed in any industry ..." | Used in the industrial-dispute and bonus parts. For wage claims, the operative word is **"employee"**. Use "employee" in letters. |

### 1.2 Are domestic workers employed by households covered? (text + our reading)

Evidence that the Code reaches household employers:
1. **s.50(4)** (records): "The provisions of sub-sections (1) to (3) shall not apply in respect of the employer to the extent he employs **not more than five persons for agriculture or domestic purpose**: Provided that such employer, when demanded, shall produce before the Inspector-cum-Facilitator, the **reasonable proof of the payment of wages** to the persons so employed." Explanation: "'domestic purpose' means the purpose exclusively relating to the home or family affairs of the employer". VERIFIED. The Code would not need a records exemption for household employers unless household employment were inside it. Even an exempt household must show "reasonable proof of the payment of wages" on demand.
2. **s.2(k)** lets the State "declare" persons to be employees.
3. **Telangana G.O.Ms.No.6 (30.05.2026)** fixes minimum wages for "employees in all the establishments defined under the Code on Wages, 2019" and lists **"DOMESTIC WORKERS" as item 51 of Schedule-II**. Schedule-III puts **"Maid"** (Unskilled item 132), **"House keeping"** (Unskilled 146), **"Sweeper"** (Unskilled 18 and 126), **"Washerman / Laundryman"** (Unskilled 125), **"Ayah and other similar categories"** (Unskilled 76) and **"Cook"** (Skilled item 17). VERIFIED (G.O. pp. 7-16).

Counter-argument an employer could raise: a home is not an "establishment" under s.2(m). We found **no High Court or Supreme Court ruling under the Code** on this (searched; nothing found). Status: **open legal question; the State's own notification treats domestic workers as covered.**

**App rule:** say "Telangana has notified a minimum wage for domestic workers (G.O.Ms.No.6, 2026)". Never say "you will win" or "the law guarantees". The DLSA lawyer decides strategy.

| Provision | Exact text |
|---|---|
| s.5 | "No employer shall pay to any employee wages less than the minimum rate of wages notified by the appropriate Government." |
| s.6(4) | the time rate may be fixed "(i) by the hour; or (ii) by the day; or (iii) by the month" |
| s.6(6)(a) | fixed by skill ("unskilled, skilled, semi-skilled and highly-skilled") "or geographical area or both" |
| s.7(1)(a) | basic rate plus a "cost of living allowance" adjusted to the cost-of-living index |

Floor wage figure: see 1.9.

### 1.4 Timely payment (s.16, s.17): VERIFIED

- s.16: wage period "daily or weekly or fortnightly or monthly", and never "more than a month".
- **s.17(1)**: wages to employees engaged on "(i) daily basis, at the end of the shift; (ii) weekly basis, on the last working day of the week, that is to say, before the weekly holiday; (iii) fortnightly basis, before the end of the second day after the end of the fortnight; (iv) **monthly basis, before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month**."
- **s.17(2)**: "Where an employee has been- (i) removed or dismissed from service; or (ii) retrenched or has resigned from service, or became unemployed due to closure of the establishment, the wages payable to him shall be paid **within two working days** of his removal, dismissal, retrenchment or, as the case may be, his resignation."
- s.17(3): the appropriate Government may set "any other time limit". s.17(4): time limits in other laws are not affected.

**Does the 2-working-day rule apply when a maid is dismissed?** s.17(2) has no exception for household employment, and s.50(4) shows household employers are inside the Code. So **if** domestic workers are covered (1.2), her earned wages are due within 2 working days of dismissal or resignation. We found no Telangana order under s.17(3) changing this (searched; not found). Status: **text VERIFIED; application to households is our reading.** The app may say: "Under the Code on Wages, wages due on dismissal should be paid within two working days (s.17(2))." It must not promise notice pay (see 1.8).

### 1.5 Deductions (s.18 to s.23): VERIFIED

- s.18(1): "there shall be **no deductions** from the wages of the employee, **except those as are authorised under this Code**." Explanation (a): "any payment made by an employee to the employer or his agent shall be deemed to be a deduction".
- s.18(2), permitted purposes (selected): (a) fines; (b) "deductions for his **absence from duty**"; (c) "damage to or loss of goods expressly entrusted to the employee for custody ... where such damage or loss is directly attributable to his neglect or default"; (d) house-accommodation supplied; (e) amenities and services authorised by Government order; (f) recovery of **advances** and "adjustment of overpayment of wages"; (h) tax and court orders; (i) social-security contributions.
- **s.18(3)**: total deductions in any wage period "shall not exceed **fifty per cent.** of such wages".
- s.19: fines only for acts and omissions specified "with the previous approval of the appropriate Government" in a displayed notice, after a chance to show cause; s.19(4) cap of "three per cent. of the wages" in a wage period. A household will practically never meet this, so **a household "fine" is almost always unlawful**.
- **s.20(2)**: an absence deduction may not be larger in proportion to the month's wages than "the period for which he was absent bears to the total period" she was required to work. So 2 days absent out of 26 working days = at most 2/26 of the month's wage.
- s.21: deduction for damage "shall not exceed the amount of the damage or loss" and only after "an opportunity of showing cause".
- s.22: deduction for housing or amenities only if "accepted by him as a term of employment" and not above their value.
- s.23(a): an advance given before employment began is recovered "from the first payment of wages"; other advance recovery "subject to such conditions as may be prescribed".

**For a maid:** cutting pay because a glass broke is lawful only within s.18(2)(c) and s.21 (entrusted goods, her neglect, actual value, after she is heard), and total deductions never above 50% of the month's wage. Cutting pay because the **family** went on holiday is not her "absence from duty" (s.20(1) is about the employee's absence "from the place ... where by the terms of his employment, he is required to work"). Holding back a month's pay as a "deposit" has no basis in s.18. Status: text VERIFIED; application is our reading.

### 1.6 Claims: who decides, who can file, time limit, compensation (s.45, s.49, s.57, s.59, s.60): VERIFIED

| Point | Exact text | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides (s.45(1)) | "The appropriate Government may, by notification, appoint one or more authorities, not below the rank of a Gazetted Officer, to hear and determine the claims which arises under the provisions of this Code." | The claim goes to a **claims authority** (a Gazetted Officer), **not** to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. For a household in Telangana the appropriate Government is the **State**. |
| Who can file (s.45(4)) | "(a) the employee concerned; or (b) any Trade Union registered under the Trade Unions Act, 1926 of which the employee is a member; or (c) the Inspector-cum-Facilitator." | The maid herself, her registered union, or the Inspector. The app cannot file in its own name. |
| Group claim (s.45(5)) | "a single application may be filed ... on behalf or in respect of any number of employees employed in an establishment" | Several workers of one employer can file together. |
| Time limit (s.45(6)) | "may be filed within a period of **three years** from the date on which claims ... arises: Provided that the authority ... may, entertain the application after three years on sufficient cause being shown" | 3 years from when each unpaid amount fell due; late filing allowed with a good reason. |
| **Compensation (s.45(2))** | "may order, **having regard to the circumstances under which the claim arises**, the payment of compensation in addition to the claim determined, which **may extend to ten times** of the claim determined and endeavour shall be made by the authority to decide the claim within a period of three months." | **Discretionary; a ceiling, not an entitlement.** The authority can give anything from 0x to 10x. The worker cannot demand it as of right. Never add "3x" or "10x" to a claim total. |
| Recovery (s.45(3)) | unpaid award -> "certificate of recovery to the Collector or District Magistrate ... who shall recover the same as arrears of land revenue" | If the employer ignores the order, the Collector recovers it like land revenue. |
| Civil-court powers (s.45(7)) | the authority has "all the powers of a civil court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, for the purpose of taking evidence ..." | It can summon the employer and documents. |
| Appeal (s.49(1)) | to the appellate authority "within ninety days from the date of such order" (late appeal on "sufficient cause") | Either side can appeal within 90 days. |
| Civil suit barred (s.57) | "No court shall entertain any suit for the recovery of minimum wages, any deduction from wages ... in so far as the sum so claimed ... (d) could have been recovered under this Code." | For a minimum-wage shortfall or illegal deductions, the route is the s.45 authority, **not** a civil suit. |
| **Burden of proof (s.59)** | "Where a claim has been filed on account of non-payment of remuneration ... or less payment of wages ... or on account of making deductions not authorised by this Code ..., the **burden to prove that the said dues have been paid shall be on the employer**." | Strong point for a maid paid in cash with no slip: the employer must prove payment. |
| **Waiver void (s.60)** | "Any contract or agreement whereby an employee relinquishes the right to any amount ... under this Code shall be null and void" | "She agreed to Rs 5,000" does not defeat the minimum wage. |

**Telangana s.45 authority:** we found **no** Telangana notification naming the s.45 authority (searched; nothing found). Telangana Code on Wages Rules exist only as a **draft** (Code on Wages (Telangana State) Rules, 2021, G.O.Rt.No.480, 29.09.2021: SNIPPET via greytHR, https://greythr.com/wiki/acts/telangana-code-on-wages-rules-2021 ; draft PDF on the department site: https://labour.telangana.gov.in/content/Downloads/code_on_wages_state_draft_rule_2021.pdf). The draft defines "Authority" as the one appointed under s.45(1) and has a single-application form under s.45(5) (SNIPPET). **App rule:** say "file a wage claim with the Telangana Labour Department (Assistant Commissioner of Labour for your area)". Do not name a specific "s.45 authority" officer.

### 1.7 Penalties (s.52 to s.56): VERIFIED, but this is not the worker's money

- s.54(1)(a): an employer who "pays to any employee less than the amount due ... shall be punishable with fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees"; a repeat within 5 years: imprisonment up to 3 months, or fine up to Rs 1 lakh, or both (s.54(1)(b)).
- s.54(3): before prosecuting for "other" contraventions, the Inspector must first give a written compliance direction.
- s.52(1): a court acts only on a complaint by the Government, an authorised officer, "an employee or a registered Trade Union ... or an Inspector-cum-Facilitator".
- s.56(1): most offences can be compounded for "fifty per cent. of the maximum fine".

**Fines go to the State, not to the worker.** The app must not add penalties to the amount owed, and must not threaten jail in a first letter.

### 1.8 What the Code does NOT give a domestic worker (do not claim these)

- **No statutory notice pay or retrenchment compensation** in the Code on Wages. (Notice and retrenchment rules sit in the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, tied to "industry"; whether a household is an "industry" is doubtful.) Do not claim "one month's notice pay" unless her agreement promised it.
- **No paid-leave entitlement** in the Code on Wages. Annual leave with wages is in the OSH Code, 2020 for establishments with 20+ workers, not a household (SNIPPET; not re-read here).
- Weekly rest: s.13(1)(b) lets the Government provide a rest day. G.O.Ms.No.6 para 5(5): work "on designated weekly rest days or public holidays shall be compensated at double (two times) the normal wage rate, subject to applicable labour rules" (VERIFIED text). Whether this binds a household with no designated rest day: **arguable; do not lead with it.**
- **Bonus** (Code Chapter IV) applies to establishments "in which twenty or more persons are employed" (VERIFIED text): not a household.
- **No "exemplary" or automatic multiple** of the claim (see s.45(2)).

## 2. Gig and platform workers (brief: not Baaki's core user)

### 2.1 Are they "employees" under the Code on Wages?
- Code on Social Security, 2020 (Act 36 of 2020), **s.2(35)**: "'gig worker' means a person who performs work or participates in a work arrangement and earns from such activities **outside of traditional employer-employee relationship**". **s.2(60)**: "'platform work' means a work arrangement outside of a traditional employer-employee relationship in which organisations or individuals use an online platform ..."; **s.2(61)**: "'platform worker' means a person engaged in or undertaking platform work". VERIFIED (PRS copy of the Gazette text, `sources/CSS2020.pdf`, https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2020/Code%20On%20Social%20Security,%202020.pdf).
- So the statute itself treats a Swiggy or Zomato delivery partner as **outside** an employer-employee relationship. The Code on Wages s.45 route is for "employees". **Applying Code on Wages wage-claim sections to a delivery partner is wrong unless a court or authority finds real employment.** (This was the competitor's mistake.)
- Telangana G.O.Ms.No.6 adds "eCommerce and Courier Service" (Schedule-I item 1) and lists "Delivery boy" and "Deliveryman" as **Semi-skilled**. That covers **employees** of such establishments (a courier firm's salaried staff), not independent app partners. Press lines such as "extends minimum wage protection to gig workers" (Outlook, 1 Jun 2026, https://www.outlookindia.com/national/telangana-bans-cash-wage-payments-extends-minimum-wage-protection-to-gig-workers) over-read the G.O. (SNIPPET; our reading.)

### 2.2 Code on Social Security, 2020: what gig and platform workers get (VERIFIED text)
- s.113(1): every unorganised, gig or platform worker "shall be required to be registered", having "completed sixteen years of age" and given a self-declaration; s.113(2): application with "Aadhaar number"; s.113(3): a registered worker is "eligible to avail the benefit of the concerned scheme".
- s.114(1): the Centre "may frame and notify ... suitable social security schemes for gig workers and platform workers" (life and disability cover, accident insurance, health and maternity, old age, creche).
- s.114(4): aggregator contribution "not exceeding two per cent., but not less than one per cent. ... of the annual turnover", and "shall not exceed five per cent. of the amount paid or payable by an aggregator to gig workers and platform workers"; s.114(5): start date "shall be notified". **Rate and start date not yet notified** as of our search (MediaNama, May 2026: https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-social-security-central-rules-2026-gig-workers-90-days-work/): SNIPPET.
- **90/120-day rule:** Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 (notified 8 May 2026): eligible if engaged **at least 90 days with one aggregator, or 120 days across several**, in the previous financial year; eligibility lapses at 60 or if the threshold is missed. SNIPPET (MediaNama). Rule number reported as Rule 48: SNIPPET.

### 2.3 Telangana Platform Based Gig Workers (Registration, Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2026: VERIFIED (Gazette text, PRS copy, `sources/TG_Act21_2026_GigWorkers.pdf`)
- **Act No. 21 of 2026**: "received the assent of the Governor on the 25th April, 2026 and the said assent is hereby first published on the 28th April, 2026 in the Telangana Gazette" (Part IV-B Extraordinary No. 211). https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/telangana/2026/Act21of2026TG.pdf
- **In force?** A G.O. reportedly appoints **2 June 2026** (SNIPPET, simpliance listing); press says rollout slipped for lack of guidelines, then "likely from Aug 15" (Deccan Chronicle: https://www.deccanchronicle.com/amp/southern-states/telangana/state-govt-finalises-guidelines-for-gig-workers-welfare-act-new-law-likely-from-aug-15-1976380). Whether the Board, Grievance Redressal Officer and portal are working today: **not confirmed**.
- s.2(r) "Termination" includes "blocking, deactivating or suspending access to his account ... irrespective of the period of such restriction".
- **s.14**: a platform may terminate "by following the principles of natural justice after due enquiry only by giving valid reason/s in writing and with prior notice of **seven days**", except immediate termination where "a threat (physical or mental) is foreseen to the end consumer".
- **s.15(1)**: the platform "must inform ... about the reasons for such deductions" and "shall mandatorily make pay-out as per the contract with no delay in disbursal of pay"; s.15(2): the welfare fee may not be deducted from the worker's pay.
- s.13(2): 14 days' notice of contract or algorithm changes. s.12(1): information "in Telugu, Hindi or any other language listed in the Eighth Schedule".
- **s.22**: the State appoints a **Grievance Redressal Officer**; a **registered** worker may apply "either in person or through web portal"; order "within thirty (30) days". **s.23**: appeal to an Appellate Authority (Deputy Commissioner rank) "within ninety (90) days". **s.24**: platforms with 100+ registered workers must run an Internal Dispute Resolution Committee (30 days). **Schedule-II** disputes include termination without notice (s.14) and failure "to make payout as per contract" or to give reasons for deductions (s.15).
- **Correct route for a delivery partner in Telangana (withheld payout or deactivation):** platform's internal committee, then the Grievance Redressal Officer under s.22 of Act 21 of 2026, then the Appellate Authority. Not a Code on Wages s.45 claim.

### 3.2 Domestic workers in G.O.Ms.No.6 - VERIFIED
- Schedule-II item 51: **"DOMESTIC WORKERS"**.
- Schedule-III designations: Unskilled: **"Maid"** (132), **"House keeping"** (146), "Sweeper" (18, 126), "Washerman / Laundryman" (125), "Cleaning of Vessels" (124), "Ayah and other similar categories" (76), "Care taker" (127), "Mali / Gardener" (15), "Watchman / Chowkidar ..." (24). Semi-skilled: "Butler / Assistant Cook" (Semi-skilled list), "Crech Ayah / Untrained Crech Attendant". Skilled: **"Cook"** (17).

## 6. A legally safe demand letter

No statute prescribes a pre-claim demand letter for wages; it is good practice and creates evidence (date of demand, employer's reply or silence). Nothing in the Code makes it a pre-condition for a s.45 claim (VERIFIED by absence). The letter is sent **by the worker, in her name**; the app is a drafting tool.

**Must contain**
1. Worker's name, the employer's name and address, date.
2. Facts only: start date, work done, agreed pay, what was paid, what is unpaid, month by month (a small table).
3. The amount demanded and how it is calculated (unpaid wages; if claiming the minimum-wage gap, show "notified minimum (G.O.Ms.No.6, Zone-I, Unskilled: Rs 16,000/month basic) minus paid").
4. A clear request: pay Rs X by a date (7 days is common) by UPI/bank to [account].
5. Polite next step: "If not paid, I will seek help from the Telangana Labour Department and the District Legal Services Authority."
6. Signature (name / thumb impression), phone. Keep a copy and proof of sending.

**Citing sections:** optional, and keep it short and correct. Safe citations: "Code on Wages, 2019: s.17 (time for payment; within two working days after dismissal), s.18 (no unauthorised deductions; 50% cap), s.5 (minimum wage), Telangana G.O.Ms.No.6 dated 30.05.2026." Do not over-cite.

**Must NOT**
- Add any **compensation multiple** (3x, 10x, "exemplary"): s.45(2) is the authority's discretion, not her claim.
- Add **fines or penalties** (s.54) to the amount, or threaten jail, police, FIR, "legal action within 24 hours", or social-media shaming. (Threats can backfire on the worker; criminal intimidation exposure: our reading.)
- Claim notice pay, bonus, paid leave, gratuity or interest that no law or agreement gives her (1.8).
- Use Code on Wages sections for app-based **gig** workers (2.1).
- Pretend to be a lawyer or a government office, use a letterhead resembling the Labour Department, or say "legal notice under section X" as if sent by an advocate. (Practice of law: see earlier research file section 7; Advocates Act 1961 reserves practice to advocates: SNIPPET.)
- State a guaranteed outcome ("you will get Rs 1,60,000").

**Disclaimer the app should show (and read aloud, in Telugu/Hindi/English):**
"Baaki is an information tool, not a lawyer and not a government office. It does not give legal advice. Amounts are estimates from the Telangana minimum wage order (G.O.Ms.No.6, 2026) and what you told us. Check with the Labour Department or the free legal aid office (DLSA, NALSA helpline 15100) before acting."

**AI voice label (IT Rules amendment, 2026).** Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026, **G.S.R. 120(E)**, notified **10 Feb 2026**, in force **20 Feb 2026**: "synthetically generated information" includes audio "artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified or altered" that appears real; audio must carry "a **prominently prefixed audio disclosure**" and "permanent metadata or other appropriate technical provenance mechanisms ... including a unique identifier"; "Transcription, translation, subtitling, dubbing" of existing content in good faith is carved out. VERIFIED via law-firm reproductions in our earlier file (`deposit-wapas/docs/research/deep/02_law_policy.md` s.6; SAM: https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf ; TeamLease reproduction: https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/52579/information-technology-intermediary-guidelines-and-digital-media-ethics-code-amendment-rules-2026/). No fixed seconds or percentage for the prefix in the final text (VERIFIED as absent there). Whether a small app is an "intermediary": open; label anyway.
- **App rule:** any TTS voice message sent to an employer starts with e.g. "ఇది AI వాయిస్ సందేశం, [worker first name] తరఫున Baaki యాప్ ద్వారా పంపబడింది" / "Yeh AI voice message hai, [name] ki taraf se, Baaki app se." Write metadata (ID) into the audio file. Never clone the employer's or the worker's voice.

## 8. Rules the app must follow (one page)

**A. Claims Baaki CAN make (with the source to show)**
1. "Telangana's minimum wage for a domestic worker (maid/cleaner, unskilled) in Hyderabad is **Rs 16,000 a month basic** from 1 June 2026; a **cook** (skilled) is **Rs 18,500**." (G.O.Ms.No.6, 30.05.2026, Table 1, Zone-I; Sch-II item 51; Sch-III "Maid", "Cook".) Add: "plus VDA when the Labour Commissioner notifies it" and "subject to pending court cases".
2. "Per day = monthly / 26." (G.O. para 5(1).) Rs 615.50 maid, Rs 711.50 cook.
3. Hourly and part-time amounts are **estimates** (monthly / 26 / 8). Label them.
4. Before June 2026: old notified rate (G.O.Ms.No.60/2011): e.g., **Rs 1,348/month for 1 hour a day; Rs 10,785/month for 8 hours** (1.4.2026-30.9.2026).
5. Monthly wages are due "before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month" (s.17(1)(iv)); on dismissal or resignation, "within two working days" (s.17(2)).
6. No deductions except those the Code allows; never more than 50% in a month (s.18(1), s.18(3)); absence cut only in proportion to days absent (s.20(2)); breakage only for her proven neglect, at actual value, after hearing her (s.18(2)(c), s.21).
7. Food/room can count for at most 15% of wages (s.2(y) Explanation).
8. An agreement to accept less than the minimum is void (s.60); the employer must prove payment (s.59).
9. She can file a claim within **3 years** (s.45(6)); she, her registered union, or the Inspector can file (s.45(4)); the authority **may** award compensation up to 10 times, at its discretion (s.45(2)).
10. Free legal aid is available to every woman (TSLSA FAQ / LSA Act s.12); NALSA helpline **15100**.

**B. Claims Baaki must NEVER make**
1. Never add 3x, 10x or "exemplary" compensation to a demand or a total.
2. Never add fines/penalties (s.54) to what she is owed; never threaten jail/police/FIR in a first letter.
3. Never apply Code on Wages claim sections to app-based gig/platform workers (CSS s.2(35), s.2(61): outside employer-employee relationship). Route them to Telangana Act 21 of 2026 (s.14, s.15, s.22).
4. Never promise notice pay, paid leave, bonus, gratuity, maternity benefit or interest for a household worker.
5. Never say "cash payment is illegal" to a household worker.
6. Never say "you will win" / "guaranteed"; never claim to be a lawyer or government office.
7. Never present hourly/part-time pro-rated figures as notified rates.
8. Never present VDA amounts for Oct 2026 onwards as fact (not notified as far as we found).
9. Never call 14434 a wage-complaint line (it is the e-Shram help desk); never send a household maid to Samadhan as the main route.
10. Never send unlabelled AI voice to an employer; never clone a voice.
